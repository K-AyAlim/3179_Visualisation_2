Purr-sonalised Care

Does breed type affect essential cat care in Australia?

This visualisation explores whether purebred and mixed breed cats differ when it comes to three essentials of responsible ownership: microchipping, desexing and keeping cats indoors. Along the way, it shows why every cat deserves this care, whatever its breed, to keep it safe and to protect the wildlife around it.

Purebred vs Mixed: Microchipping

Registered pedigree breeders must microchip every kitten before it leaves their care, so most purebreds start life chipped. Many other cats are not, with only 9% of stray cats admitted to RSPCA Queensland shelters carrying a microchip. Whatever its breed, a microchip is what brings a lost cat home.

Why It Still Matters

Cats get lost and end up in shelters

Thankfully, most cats that enter the Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA) shelters are either rehomed or reunited with their owners. However, a cat can only be returned if it can be identified, which is why the RSPCA states that microchips are the best way for a lost pet to be returned home.

But older cats wait longer...

Kittens are adopted quickly, whereas adolescent and adult cats are almost twice as likely to wait more than 60 days for a new home (20% compared with 11%). This delay carries real consequences. Pounds are only required to hold a lost cat for 3 to 14 days depending on the state.

Although most shelters strive to be no kill, every additional cat adds pressure, and for an unidentified cat a single day can be the difference between life and death.

...but some never make it back

In 2023 to 2024, the RSPCA euthanised 5,148 cats. Medical reasons accounted for 41% of these cases and behavioural reasons for 35%.

Some of these outcomes could have been prevented had the cats been microchipped, as identified cats can be returned home before their condition worsens.

Not to mention the RSPCA is only one shelter provider. Across Australia, PetRescue estimates that around 50,000 cats are killed each year in the care of councils, shelters and rescue groups.

4Essential Care: Desexing /ᐠ｡ꞈ｡ᐟ\

Desexing is the act of spaying or neutering a cat in order to surgically sterilise them, preventing unwanted litters and certain health issues. Are purebred cats desexed differently from mixed breed cats? And why does desexing every cat matter?

Purebred vs Mixed

Hidden Differences

Purebred and mixed breed cats seen by Australian vets are desexed at almost the same rate, 83.4% of purebreds compared with 84.8% of mixed breeds.

The real difference lies in when they are desexed. Only 15% of desexed purebreds had the procedure by four months of age, compared with 25% of mixed breed cats.

This timing matters, as cats can begin breeding from as young as four months, often before their owners expect it. Desexing a kitten early, regardless of its breed, is the most reliable way to prevent unplanned litters before they happen.

Why It Still Matters

The Feral Consequence

Each dot on this map is a recorded cat sighting, with almost 69,000 records spread across every state and territory. Clusters near cities and roads reflect where people are most likely to report them, yet the sightings stretch deep into remote Australia, where few people live.

The Invasive Species Council estimates there could be as many as 5.6 million feral cats in the Australian bush after wet years. These cats are descended from pets that were abandoned or never desexed, and every unplanned litter adds to a population that is nearly impossible to remove once it has spread.

Ruinous Impact on Diversity

Australian feral cats kill an estimated 1.15 billion mammales, 679 million reptlies and 434 million birds yearly. That is an impact of 2 billion critters killed, a lot of them being native species.

Thus, desexing pet cats is one of the most effective ways to stop this population from growing and annually gutting our ecosystem.

Desexing on a Budget

Desexing can be a significant expense. On average it costs between $185 in Tasmania and $245 in New South Wales. This can be a lot of money to spend in a single trip.

Fortunately, help is available. The National Desexing Network connects owners with participating vets that offer discounted desexing, and many local councils run subsidised desexing programs, particularly for pensioners and low income households.

Purebred vs Mixed: Keeping Cats Indoors

Pedigree cats are about four times more likely to be kept indoors than mixed breeds, yet every cat faces the same dangers outside. Keeping any cat indoors protects both the cat and the native wildlife around it.

Why It Still Matters

Ecosystem Annihilators

Regardless of breed type, on average, each roaming pet cat kills 110 native animals a year: 40 reptiles, 38 birds and 32 mammals. At the time of the study (2023), 2.7 million (71%) of Australia’s then 3.8 million pet cats were allowed to roam, and together they killed an estimated 241 million native animals, with devastating effects on local ecosystems.

Keeping cats contained protects wildlife as much as it protects the cats themselves.

Don't worry, you’re not alone

Containing a cat is a widely supported choice. In a 2024 survey of South Australians, 72% supported keeping cats contained, while only 10% opposed it.

Their safety is in YOUR hands!

Keep your cat safe by microchipping, desexing and keeping them indoors, whatever their breed!
