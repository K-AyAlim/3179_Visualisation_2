Fur Real: Who Are Australia's Cats?

Exploring the ups and downs of domestic cat breeds in Australia

Purebred cats are beautiful, but the reality is that most cats in Australia aren’t purebred. What truly shapes their lives isn’t their breed, but how we care for them. So let’s look past the pedigree and into the realities of cat life in Australia, from the homes they share with us to the shelters some end up in, and how we can keep them safe.

How popular are cats in Australia?

1 in 3 homes has a cat!

The 2025 Pet Ownership Study revealed that cats are the second most popular pet in Australia, so much so that 33% of all households in the country have a purring pet.

Most cats aren’t purebred, but they are all claw-some! In this section we will breakdown the breed frequencies in the places that publish their data and explore that while purebreds get a lot of attention, they are a much smaller part than you think.

People have favourites

Among the cats that do have a pedigree, each town has its own taste. In Townsville more than a third of purebreds are Ragdolls, Surf Coast owners love their Burmese, and Golden Plains leans towards the British Shorthair. Hover over a square to see how many cats it stands for.

But out of 100 cats, more than 4 in 5 have no pedigree!

Look at all the registered cats in these three councils, though, and the purebreds only fill 13 squares. The rest are mixed, crossbred or of unknown breed: the everyday moggies that most of us actually share our homes with.

Microchipping, registering and desexing cats is a level of care every responsible owner owes to their pet. Let’s explore what happens when cats get lost and end up roaming, from ending up in shelters to threatening wildlife.

Thankfully most make it home!

Most cats that end up in shelters are rehomed or reunited with their owners, though the share changes from state to state.

Map: % of cats rehomed by state

But older cats wait longer

Kittens are snapped up quickly, but adolescent and adult cats are almost twice as likely to wait more than 60 days for a new home. Every cat that is desexed and microchipped is one less unplanned litter, and one less cat waiting in a shelter.

Oh no: not every cat makes it

In 2023–24 the RSPCA euthanised 5,148 cats. Most were for medical or behavioural reasons, many of which are linked to cats that roamed, fought, bred or were never vaccinated.

It matters for wildlife too

A pet cat that roams kills around 110 native animals a year: 40 reptiles, 38 birds and 32 mammals. Keeping cats safe at home protects our wildlife as much as it protects them.
